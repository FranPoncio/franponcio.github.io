## The context

Seven years in public gas infrastructure in Córdoba. Other people built the works
—that side is in [End-to-end works management](/en/projects/obra-gas/)—; **my job
was knowing the state of everything and making it reportable** to the
organisations financing it, under their standards.

The network takes in trunk pipelines, distribution branch lines, pressure
regulating stations and industrial connections. Every asset has its own cycle: it
gets designed, tendered, built, commissioned, operated.

<TrazaGas lang="en" />

## The problem

The data existed, but split across inspection, administration and operations —
each area with its own cut-off dates and its own definition of progress.

When two areas reported different progress for the same job, it was almost never
a data-entry error. **It was that each one understood "progress" differently.**
That observation set up everything else: the problem was not about tools.

## What I built

- **Data model and indicators.** Defining what gets measured, at what
  granularity and against what baseline. `[To complete: the concrete decision you
  made about the unit of measurement.]`

- **Physical and financial progress, kept apart.** 40 % of pipe laid is not 40 %
  of budget spent. The gap between the two curves is the information, not an
  error to be corrected.

- **The IDB / IFC standard.** Reporting under a multilateral organisation's
  standard shapes the whole model: what gets recorded, with what supporting
  evidence, and how it is demonstrated. **This is the part that sets you apart** —
  plenty of people do Power BI, very few have reported under these rules.

- **ETL and dashboards.** Ingestion from source systems with **SQL**, published
  in **Power BI** and **Tableau**. It is the same craft I had been doing since
  the warehouse in 2013, now over 12,000 km of network.
