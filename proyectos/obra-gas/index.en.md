## Context

Seven years in public gas infrastructure in Córdoba, covering the full life of a
project: **before, during and after**. That's a path normally split across three
different departments, and here it ran through one place.

That's the point of this case. Someone who writes a tender package without ever
having supervised a site writes specifications that can't be met. Someone who
supervises without having written the package doesn't know what was negotiated,
or why.

## Before the works

### Project definition

Scope, route and technical solutions. This is the stage where you decide the
things that can't be changed later without renegotiating the contract.

### The tender package

Preparing all the documentation to go to tender: specifications, bills of
quantities, budget, drawings. An ambiguous specification doesn't get paid for at
tender — it gets paid for during execution, in variation orders and in arguments
nobody can win because the paper doesn't say.

### Tendering and contracting

Full public tendering process, bid analysis and contracting. Here traceability
isn't a virtue, it's a legal requirement — and it shapes how every decision gets
documented.

## During the works

Supervision and management across four disciplines on the same route:

- **Mechanical.** The pipeline itself: welding, non-destructive testing,
  pressure testing, backfill.
- **Civil.** Surface facilities — foundations, enclosures, crossing works.
- **Electronic.** Fibre optics for telemetry and control. It's the least visible
  discipline and the one that most shapes operations afterwards: without data,
  the network runs blind.
- **Environmental.** Field compliance, soil management and reinstatement.

**The hard part isn't any one of the four — it's the sequence.** Each depends on
the previous one having finished properly, and a mechanical defect surfaces once
the civil crew has already backfilled.

## LNG and plants

Beyond the pipeline itself: **pressure reduction stations** and **liquefied
natural gas** interventions, including compression at industrial plants.

LNG changes the problem. It isn't just moving gas — it's a change of state with
its own temperature, pressure and safety conditions. A pipeline feeding an
industrial plant and an LNG facility share little beyond the molecule.

## Alternative energy

Environmental work on **biodiesel and biogas**. That's where the energy mix is
heading, and it shows up in the same place as traditional infrastructure: the
existing network is what will carry whatever comes next.

## Outcome

Seven years, **more than 35 projects** taken from the tender package through to
commissioning, and **320 km of route worked** across pipelines and branch lines.
Individual projects ran **6 to 18 months** depending on the section and how many
crossings it had.

What those numbers don't show is the part that took the most work: none of those
projects stalled on an argument between disciplines that the tender package
should have settled.

## What I took from it

That projects don't fail on the technical side. They fail at the joints: between
the specification and the execution, between one discipline and the next, between
what was contracted and what was understood. **Having stood at both ends is what
lets you see those joints before they open.**
